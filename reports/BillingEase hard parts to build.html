The hard parts of BillingEase are not invoices, quotes or reports. They are the five features that move money or file with a government: payroll, online payments and bill pay, US sales tax filing, mandatory e-invoicing, and bank feeds. Licensing, personal liability and monthly rule changes make each of them hard, more than the code does. Each one can be bought from a licensed vendor in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and Singapore. Coverage is patchy in India and the EU and almost absent in Sri Lanka, so what BillingEase can offer at launch differs sharply by country. The recommended plan has four parts. First, build in-house the accounting core: an append-only Postgres ledger, multi-currency handling, single-rate VAT/GST logic, receipt capture, the HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD) integration and accountant workflows. Second, buy everything regulated: Stripe Connect with hosted checkout and Stripe bearing losses, embedded payroll partners, bank-data aggregators, a US tax engine plus a filing partner, and Peppol access points. Third, launch first in the US, UK and Canada. Fourth, add Australia and Singapore next, and India, Sri Lanka and the EU (beyond basic invoicing) only once local partners are signed. Wave proves the business model: free software funded by payments and payroll revenue. It also shows the scale involved. Wave had over 200 staff when H&R Block bought it in 2019 (Glenbrook), and it still limited full-service payroll tax filing to a subset of US states for years (FitSmallBusiness). One caveat runs through this report: the researchers could read only search-result snippets, not the vendor pages themselves, so every price below must be re-checked on the live vendor page before it goes into a budget.

Payments: Stripe Connect covers six markets, while India and Sri Lanka need local aggregators

Why it is hard, and where the licensing line sits

Invoice payments look like a checkout button. The difficulty is that the moment BillingEase takes possession or control of a customer's money, it becomes a regulated money transmitter. In the US, FinCEN's payment-processor exemption requires four conditions. The business must facilitate purchases or bill payments, settle only through systems that admit BSA-regulated institutions, and hold a formal agreement with the seller (Gunster). The states are a separate layer: money transmission needs a licence in 49 states plus DC, with Montana the exception (private.law). The "agent of payee" exemption that payment facilitators rely on is recognised in only about half of the states (Venable, 2018). California's financial regulator has ruled that a payment processing service did not qualify (CA DFPI). In the UK and EU, the PSD2 "commercial agent" exclusion now covers only agents acting for one side of a transaction, which shuts out two-sided platforms. Such a platform needs its own payment-institution or e-money licence, or a licensed provider such as Stripe Connect that takes regulatory responsibility (Womble Bond Dickinson; Ryft).

The Asian markets are stricter. India's RBI Payment Aggregator Directions of 15 September 2025 require ₹15 crore net worth at application, rising to ₹25 crore by the end of the third year. Non-bank aggregators had to apply by 31 December 2025 (RBI; Inc42). Singapore's Payment Services Act licenses "merchant acquisition service" whether or not the provider ever holds the money (Duane Morris). Even a non-custodial integration there should get a counsel check. Sri Lanka regulates aggregators under CBSL General Direction No. 1 of 2018, and PayHere was the first gateway approved under it (Daily FT).

Card-data security (PCI DSS) is the final constraint. A merchant qualifies for the lightest self-assessment form (SAQ A) only if every element of the payment page comes from a PCI-compliant provider. Mixing in any self-hosted element moves it to SAQ A-EP (PCI SSC). So the invoice pay page should use Stripe Checkout or Stripe-hosted elements, never a home-built card field.

Vendors and prices by market

Stripe Connect is the only provider in the notes that covers the US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia and Singapore on one connected-account model, and it has two pricing modes. In mode A, "Stripe handles pricing", Stripe bills the businesses directly and the platform pays no Connect fees (Stripe). In mode B, "You handle pricing", the platform sets its own rate and pays $2 per monthly active account plus 0.25% + 25¢ per payout. Instant payouts cost 1% and cross-border payouts 0.25% (Stripe US). An account counts as active only in months when it receives a payout (drop-desk). The loss-bearer choice matters most. In Stripe's account configuration, losses_collector set to stripe or application decides who absorbs negative balances. Choosing application also makes the platform the fee collector, and the setting cannot be changed after the account is created (Stripe docs).

Stripe India has been invite-only for new accounts since May 2024 (Stripe support), and a secondary source says it lacks UPI and wallets (PayGlocal). India therefore needs Razorpay Route, which splits funds to sellers or sub-accounts. Under the 2025 RBI directions, though, a business must meet minimum turnover thresholds to get or keep Route access (Razorpay docs). For Singapore, HitPay charges 0.65% + S$0.30 on PayNow online transactions of S$100 and above, 2.8% + S$0.50 on domestic cards and 3.65% + S$0.50 on international cards (HitPay). HitPay says it holds a MAS Major Payment Institution licence, which has not been checked against the MAS register. Sri Lanka's PayHere charged 2.9–3.9% in 2019 (readme.lk); that figure is dated, and no current price or platform-split support was found. Adyen for Platforms is a poor launch fit, with a reported minimum monthly invoice of about $120 and typical volumes of $1M+ a year (costbench). For bank debits, GoCardless UK charges 1% + 20p capped at £4 on its Standard plan (dodopayments). Its USD pricing is quoted inconsistently (erpresearch; toolradar).

The competitor benchmark is consistent. Wave charges 2.9% + 60¢ on cards and 1% (minimum $1) on ACH (Wave support). FreshBooks charges 2.9% + 30¢ on domestic consumer cards (paymentreview), and Xero passes through Stripe and GoCardless rather than processing payments itself (feeflowcalculator). Wave's model is to fund free software with payments and payroll fees. H&R Block bought it for $405M in cash and expected $40–45M of FY2020 revenue (H&R Block 8-K; BetaKit). Other sources put the price at about US$398M (Glenbrook).

The margin trap in "you handle pricing"

Stripe's sourced fee schedule shows that copying Wave's price on Stripe's mode B does not obviously make money at small-business volumes (est., this report's arithmetic). Take a business invoicing $5,000 a month as ten $500 invoices, paid out in four payouts. Charging Wave's 2.9% + 60¢ against Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ cost earns $3.00 of spread. Mode B costs $15.50: $2 account fee, plus 0.25% of $5,000 ($12.50), plus four 25¢ payout fees. The 0.25% payout fee scales with volume while a fixed-fee markup does not, so a profitable mode-B price needs a percentage markup of roughly 0.3% or more, or negotiated Stripe rates. Wave's actual cost basis is unknown. The practical consequence is to launch on mode A, which carries no platform fees and keeps Stripe as loss-bearer, accept little payments revenue at first, and negotiate mode B or a revenue share once volume gives leverage. No official partner revenue-share schedule was found for any provider, so those terms will be negotiated.

Payroll is the deepest liability trap, and only four markets have embeddable engines

Why payroll ranks hardest

Payroll pairs heavy tax content with liability that falls on the employer. In the US, late federal deposits draw 2% (1–5 days), 5% (6–15 days) or 10% (more than 15 days), rising to 15% after IRS notices (IRS). The Trust Fund Recovery Penalty can reach 100% of unpaid withheld tax and is assessed on individuals personally (Paylocity). A standard reporting agent, authorised on IRS Form 8655, files and deposits for the employer but takes on no liability. It must remind clients at least quarterly that they remain liable (IRS; ADP). Liability shifts only under a Section 3504 agent (Form 2678) or a certified PEO (IRS). Moving wages raises the same money-transmitter question as payments. California exempts delivering wages and paying payroll taxes, but its regulator found licensing applied to a provider that pooled client funds (CA DFPI). Minnesota exempts payroll processors outright (Minn. Stat. 53B.29). Canada is stricter on timing: late remittances cost 3–10%, up to 20% for repeat failures, even if the payment was started on time (TaxTips.ca).

The evidence for buying is indirect but consistent. FreshBooks announced Gusto-powered US payroll in October 2023 and launched it in January 2024, for US customers only (FreshBooks). Xero has run native payroll only in AU, NZ and the UK, and has used Gusto in the US since 2018 (hr.software). Wave long offered full-service tax filing in only 14 states (FitSmallBusiness), though one aggregator says signups after April 2025 get every state (Software Finder). No source puts a number on an in-house US build. The research team's judgement is "multiple years with a dedicated tax-content team" est..

Market by market

In the US, Gusto Embedded, Check, Salsa, Zeal and Rollfi all cover 50 states, and none publishes a partner rate card (SourceForge; Open Banking Tracker). One third-party benchmark puts embedded payroll at $35–70 per company per month plus $6–10 per employee, with the platform keeping about two-thirds (FintechSpecs). Retail prices sit close to that range: Wave charges $40/month + $6/person for full service (Wave), and QuickBooks Payroll Core about $50 + $6.50 (low-quality sources) (Dancing Numbers). It is unclear whether the $35–70 benchmark is a wholesale or a retail price. If it is wholesale, unit margins at Wave's price are thin. Get quotes before modelling revenue. A third-party directory reports that Gusto partners can go live "in as little as four weeks" with pre-built flows (Open Banking Tracker).

Canada has a single integration path through Salsa (US + Canada) or Nmbr (Canada only, CAD $7.6M raised) (Salsa; BusinessWire). Wave's Canadian payroll does not support Quebec (LedgerLogic), and leaving Quebec out at launch is a precedent BillingEase can follow. The UK is the most tractable non-US market. HMRC's Real Time Information system is one central channel, and employer National Insurance has been 15% since April 2025 (PwC). HMRC-recognised payroll APIs exist: Staffology exposes payment-submission (FPS) creation and the XML before it is filed (Staffology), and Moonworkers prices per payslip (Moonworkers). Australia requires reporting every pay event to the tax office (Single Touch Payroll), and from 1 July 2026 "Payday Super" requires superannuation to reach the fund within 7 days of each payday (ATO). Pay rules under the industry awards make an in-house engine unrealistic. Employment Hero (formerly KeyPay) is the white-label engine behind more than 750 partners (Employment Hero; KeyPay).

The other markets have no embeddable engine. The EU has no single payroll: even PayFit runs native payroll only in France, Spain and the UK (hr.software), so the realistic product is importing payroll journals from DATEV, Silae or PayFit. India layers central schemes (provident fund 12% + 12%, ESI 0.75% + 3.25%) over state professional tax (Wisemonk), and RazorpayX Payroll sells at ₹150 per extra employee per month (Razorpay). No embedded API was found and local pricing leaves little room, so sync payroll journals from local providers. Sri Lanka is simple to calculate: provident fund (EPF) 8% employee + 12% employer, trust fund (ETF) 3% employer (Playroll), and personal relief raised to LKR 1.8M from April 2025 (sl-salary-cal). There is no filing API, so build a calculator, payslips and schedules, and leave filing manual. Singapore is rules-driven: CPF is 37% for employees aged 55 and under, with an ordinary wage ceiling of S$8,000 from 2026 (Harvest Accounting), and the skills levy is 0.25% capped at S$11.25 (Slasify). No payroll API provider was identified, so a calculation-only in-house build is plausible.

Sales tax, VAT and e-invoicing: a US engine, an MTD integration and Peppol partners

US sales tax is a moving target that every competitor buys or skips

Economic nexus, the post-Wayfair rule that makes out-of-state sellers collect tax once sales pass a state's threshold, keeps shifting. Sixteen states had dropped the 200-transaction test by 1 January 2026 (Avalara). Kentucky followed on 1 August 2026 (beancount.io), and Connecticut still requires both tests together (Kintsugi). Wave makes no attempt at this. Users type in each rate and Wave books a liability account (Wave help), with no nexus tracking or multistate filing (Commenda). QuickBooks Online calculates rates from business and customer addresses (Cleverence). Wave parity is therefore cheap; QuickBooks parity is a vendor contract.

The UK's MTD for Income Tax is an opportunity, not just a compliance cost

UK VAT returns must go through HMRC's MTD API. Software suppliers carry the legal duty to send fraud-prevention headers, and HMRC grants production credentials for MTD Income Tax only to software that meets its transaction-monitoring rules (HMRC directions). HMRC certifies software directly rather than intermediaries, so this is a build, at about 1–3 months for MTD VAT including recognition est.. The commercial point matters more. MTD for Income Tax began on 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income above £50,000, falling to £30,000 in 2027 and £20,000 in 2028 (GOV.UK). Each step-down creates a wave of sole traders who must buy quarterly-update software, which is exactly Wave's customer profile. MTD for Corporation Tax will not go ahead (Sage).

EU, Singapore, India and Sri Lanka mandates bunch up between 2026 and 2031

E-invoicing is what makes localisation hard. In Europe, Belgium has required Peppol for domestic B2B invoices since 1 January 2026 (SPS Commerce). Poland's KSeF clearance system covers all VAT payers from April 2026 and micro-businesses from January 2027 (invoicenavigator). France has required every business to receive e-invoices since 1 September 2026, and SMEs must issue them from 1 September 2027 through an accredited platform (plateforme agréée), with €50 fines per non-compliant invoice (SoftCo; Banqup). Germany requires receiving now and issuing from 2027 (turnover above €800K) and 2028 (everyone), and it does not mandate Peppol (e-invoicing.org). The EU's ViDA package adds intra-EU digital reporting from July 2030 (originstamp).

Elsewhere, Singapore's GST InvoiceNow (Peppol) has applied to all new voluntary GST registrants since 1 April 2026. It extends to existing businesses in tiers from April 2028 (supplies of S$200K or less) to April 2031 (above S$4M) (IRAS). India requires a registration number (IRN) from the government invoice portal for businesses with turnover above ₹5 crore, and an invoice without one is invalid (e-invoice.app). The GSTR-3B monthly return is now hard-locked to auto-populated figures (EY India). Most Indian SMBs fall below the ₹5 crore line (Tally), so India's launch blocker is the scope of GST returns, not e-invoicing. Sri Lanka began piloting invoice submission from business software to the revenue department's RAMIS system in May 2026, mandated a revised VAT invoice format from 1 July 2026 (VATupdate), and moved its VAT registration threshold during the year, with sources conflicting on the new figure (Kintsugi). Canada has no e-invoicing, and Australia's Peppol remains voluntary for B2B (fiscal-requirements).

Bank feeds: aggregators exist everywhere except Sri Lanka, but none publishes simple prices

Why feeds are hard even when bought

Feeds fail operationally rather than legally. UK rules require the data provider to reconfirm consent with the customer every 90 days, and the EU requires strong authentication every 180 days (Open Banking Ltd; Vixio). Yapily treats communicating these reconfirmations as the app's job (Yapily). India's industry body Sahamati ran a dedicated session in September 2025 on improving account-aggregator success rates (Sahamati). No published breakage or connection-success rates were found for any aggregator, so connection health, re-link prompts and a file-import fallback should be first-class features.

US regulation is in flux. The CFPB's Section 1033 open-banking rule is under a preliminary injunction, its April 2026 compliance date passed unenforced (Cozen O'Connor), and a reconsideration proposal went to OMB review in August 2026 (Consumer Finance Monitor). Meanwhile banks have started charging aggregators for data: JPMorgan's contracts covered more than 95% of the data pulled from its systems by November 2025 (PaymentExpert). Budget for aggregator price rises at renewal. Canada published proposed consumer-driven banking regulations on 27 June 2026, under Bank of Canada oversight (Bennett Jones), so credential-based aggregators remain the path for now. Sri Lanka's open-banking framework has not progressed beyond a 2020 consultation (CBSL).

The accounting core is where BillingEase should spend its own engineering

Ledger and multi-currency: build, because no vendor sells bookkeeping

Hosted and open-source ledgers solve a different problem. Modern Treasury enforces double-entry, auditability and immutability: a correction reverses the original and reposts it rather than editing it (Modern Treasury). Formance and TigerBeetle are foundations rather than finished ledger products, and a comparison notes that Modern Treasury needs "heavier lifting to deliver full bookkeeping" (fintechspecs). None of them provides accrual and cash reports, localised charts of accounts or accountant workflows. Accrual matters legally too: India's Companies Act s.128 requires books on an accrual, double-entry basis (advocatekhoj). The recommendation is an append-only Postgres ledger with balanced-journal constraints, period locks with accountant overrides, and an audit log. That takes about 2–3 senior engineers × 3–4 months, and a hosted ledger would save only 4–6 weeks of it est.. Per-country chart-of-accounts templates take about 1–2 accountant-weeks each est..

Multi-currency is harder than calling an FX API, because tax authorities name their own rates. HMRC publishes monthly rates that traders must use for VAT returns (HMRC CFM12070). The CRA accepts Bank of Canada rates (Canada.ca). Under EU VAT law the rate is the member state's market rate or the ECB rate (Directive 2006/112/EC Art. 91), and Germany uses monthly rates published by its finance ministry (Stripe). The ledger must therefore store several rate sources per date, record which one each transaction used, and post realised and unrealised gains automatically. That is about 4–6 engineer-weeks est..

Receipts, categorisation, security, messaging and migration are buildable

Receipt capture is now cheap. AWS Textract's expense analysis and Azure's prebuilt receipt models cost about $10 per 1,000 pages (wring.co; LiteLLM docs). Mistral OCR is listed at $1–2 per 1,000 pages (aiproductivity.ai). Specialist vendors cost more: Mindee is €44–584 a month with €0.035–0.05 overage per credit (Mindee), and Veryfi has a minimum of about $500/month (erpresearch). LLMs compete on accuracy: one benchmark reported a 97% receipt success rate for Claude 3.5 Sonnet (AIMultiple). The recommended design is a cheap model first, confidence scoring, user confirmation and an email-in address, at about 2–3 engineer-months est.. Categorisation follows the same pattern: per-business rules, then an LLM fallback that learns from corrections, at about 4–8 engineer-weeks est.. Ntropy offers 2,000 free test transactions but no public rate (Ntropy).

Security and privacy set dates the launch plan must respect. A first SOC 2 Type II audit needs 3–6 months of preparation plus a 3–12 month observation window. A lean first year costs about $15K–50K, or $75K–150K+ with consultants; most of these sources are vendor-affiliated (guptadeepak; startupdefense). Sri Lanka's data protection law (PDPA) imposes controller and processor obligations from 1 January 2027 (FT.lk). India's DPDP core obligations apply from about 13 May 2027, with sources disagreeing by a day (AZB Partners). Retention runs from 5 years in Australia (ATO) and 6 years at the CRA (Montreal Financial) to 8 years under India's Companies Act (Tally). Default to 8 years, and handle erasure requests by restricting the data and keeping it until retention ends.

Email has to meet the Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe and a spam rate below 0.3% (Bird). It costs $0.10 per 1,000 on SES and $15–18 per 10K on Postmark (costbench; automationatlas). WhatsApp has charged per template message since July 2025, about ₹0.13 for an Indian utility message such as a payment reminder (Meta; MyOperator), and it is the right reminder channel for India and Sri Lanka.

Migration got more expensive in 2026. Since 2 March 2026 Xero has charged app partners by connections and data egress, and its Journals endpoint requires the Advanced tier at about $895/month (Xero; Codat). Intuit has metered QuickBooks read calls since July 2025, from a free 500K-credit tier up to $4,500/month (Apideck). CSV import of contacts, items, opening balances and trial balances is the universal fallback, and the only route for Wave users. The marketing site is the easy part: Next.js with Sanity (free, then $15 per seat) or Payload (self-hosted for $20–50/month) (colorwhistle), with Contentful's jump to about $300/month as the cost to avoid (dev.to).

Build order: core first, then money rails by market readiness

The order follows two constraints: what can be bought in each market, and the dependencies between features (the ledger before reconciliation, payments before bill pay). All timings are estimates.

Phase 1, about months 0–6, US, UK and Canada. Build the ledger, invoices and quotes, multi-currency with official rates, user-entered tax rates, receipt capture, CSV import from Wave, QuickBooks and Xero, email delivery, MFA and role-based access, and the marketing site. Integrate Stripe Connect on mode A with hosted checkout, which keeps BillingEase in SAQ A and has Stripe bearing losses. Add Plaid in the US and a UK aggregator, and start SOC 2 preparation on day one so the observation window runs in parallel. These three markets come first for three reasons: every row of the matrix has a vendor; Canada is Wave's home market; and UK MTD for Income Tax creates a forced-buying moment for sole traders in 2026–2028.

Phase 2, about months 6–12. Add the HMRC MTD VAT and Income Tax integrations. Embed payroll through Salsa, which covers the US and Canada with one partner (excluding Quebec), or through Gusto Embedded or Check. Offer a US sales-tax engine and filing as a paid add-on. Add GoCardless bank debits, plus Australia with Stripe, Basiq and an Employment Hero conversation. Australia waits until phase 2 because Payday Super and awards make its payroll the hardest of the four embeddable markets.

Phase 3, about months 12–18. Launch Singapore (Stripe or HitPay, Finverse plus file import, an InvoiceNow access point, a CPF calculator), and the EU with a Peppol access-point partner, starting where Peppol is mandatory or Germany's XRechnung applies. UK payroll can arrive here through Staffology or PayRun.io if UK demand is strong. Sign a French accredited-platform partner before France's SME issuance date of September 2027.

Phase 4, about months 12–24, India and Sri Lanka. Launch India with Razorpay, a GST intermediary for returns and e-invoicing, an account-aggregator feed and DPDP compliance by May 2027. Launch Sri Lanka with PayHere, CSV/PDF/email-in statements, a payroll calculator, data-driven VAT and SSCL tables, and PDPA compliance by 1 January 2027. Bill pay comes last, through a licensed partner.

There is a case for a different order. An India or Sri Lanka engineering team costs a third or less of a US one est., and both markets favour WhatsApp-first, invoicing-led products that BillingEase could ship without payroll or feeds. The case against is commercial. In India, local payroll at ₹100–150 per employee and entrenched local products such as Zoho Books and Tally, mentioned in the notes as setting market expectations, leave little room for a payments-funded free tier. In Sri Lanka there are no feeds, card fees are high and the tax rules are still moving. Building there is sensible; launching there first is not.

Conclusion

The research changes what "hard" means for BillingEase. The engineering-heavy pieces (ledger, multi-currency, receipts, categorisation, migration) are well understood and cost a few engineer-months each. The real constraints are legal and commercial. The loss-bearer setting on each Stripe account is permanent. A Wave-style fixed-fee markup does not cover Stripe's percentage payout fee. Employers stay liable for payroll deposits a partner makes. And the 2026–2031 e-invoicing timetable will force Peppol and French accredited-platform partnerships whether or not BillingEase targets those countries early. The decisions that matter most are contract terms (who bears losses, who pays penalties caused by provider error, volume discounts on feeds), so legal and partnership work should start in parallel with phase 1 engineering, not after it.

The second change is in how to sequence markets. The useful question is how much of the product can be bought in each country, not how big the country is. On that measure the US, UK and Canada are nearly complete on day one. Australia and Singapore are close behind. India, Sri Lanka and most of the EU will run as invoicing-and-bookkeeping products with manual payroll and feeds for a long time. They need positioning, pricing and support cost models built for a lighter product rather than a cut-down Wave clone. And UK MTD for Income Tax, with its £50K, £30K and £20K steps, is the clearest near-term growth opening for a product of this kind.
